The dial

Fourteen stops. One for every decade since 1900, and one for today.

Each stop makes the live viewfinder, and the photo you save, look like that decade's everyday photo medium.

  1. 1900s Dry plate
  2. 1910s Autochrome
  3. 1920s 35 mm B&W
  4. 1930s Newsreel B&W
  5. 1940s Press B&W
  6. 1950s Slide color
  7. 1960s 8 mm film
  8. 1970s Faded print
  9. 1980s 35 mm color
  10. 1990s Camcorder tape
  11. 2000s Early digicam
  12. 2010s Smartphone
  13. 2020s Computational
  14. 2026 Today, as it is

What it does

The whole camera travels with the dial.

  • Live looks, saved as seen

    Real-time GPU shaders drive the viewfinder: color lookup tables, grain, vignette, halation, soft focus, dust, light leaks, scanlines. Stills run through the same shaders, so what you see is what you save.

  • Degree, 0 to 10

    One slider sets how strong the look is. 0 is subtle, 10 is the maximum. Grain, dust and light leaks ramp up late, so low degrees stay clean.

  • Controls on a timeline

    Flash and the self-timer appear in the 1930s. Zoom in the 1960s. Date stamps in the 1990s and 2000s. A grid in the 2010s. Exposure and the front camera work in every decade.

  • Shutter sounds by era

    Synthesized shutter sounds change as you turn the dial, from a plate-camera clack to a digicam beep.

  • Instant prints

    Flip the Frame switch and the shot ejects and develops as an instant print: a square photo on a white 4:5 card, ready for social posts. Type a one-line note and it lands in handwriting.

  • Gallery and sharing

    Photos save as JPEG to Pictures/GenerationCamera. Swipe through them in the built-in gallery and share with the Android share sheet. Photos only, no video.

Privacy

No internet permission. Your photos stay on your device.

The app works fully offline and cannot send anything anywhere. No account, no ads, no analytics, no trackers. A photo leaves your device only when you tap Share.
